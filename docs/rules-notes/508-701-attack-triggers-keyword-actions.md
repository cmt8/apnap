# Attack triggers, tokens' names, exile, mill, locked-in effects, "where X is"

Verbatim from the Comprehensive Rules effective 2026-11-06, for the M4 card batch of
decisions.md D-041. Tests: `crates/harness/tests/cards_precon.rs` (one per card).

## Attack triggers (CR 508)
> 508.1m Any abilities that trigger on attackers being declared trigger.

> 508.3a An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] attacks, . . .” triggers if that
> creature is declared as an attacker. Similarly, “Whenever [a creature] attacks [a player,
> planeswalker, or battle], . . .” triggers if that creature is declared as an attacker
> attacking that player or permanent. Such abilities won’t trigger if a creature is put onto
> the battlefield attacking.

Engine: `triggers::detect_attacks` runs once, from the declaration (`DeclareAttackersFrame`),
over the creatures that were declared — so a creature put onto the battlefield attacking
(not in the pool yet) would not trigger it. The triggers go on the stack when a player
would next get priority, in the declare attackers step.

## Token names (CR 111.4)
> 111.4. A spell or ability that creates a token sets both its name and its subtype(s). If
> the spell or ability doesn’t specify the name of the token, its name is the same as its
> subtype(s) plus the word “Token.” Once a token is on the battlefield, changing its name
> doesn’t change its subtype(s), and vice versa.

So a "2/2 black Zombie creature token" is named **Zombie Token** (subtype Zombie). Token
definitions carry that name; scripts refer to them by `key`, because different tokens share
a name (a 5/5 and a 6/6 "Dragon Token").

## Keyword actions (CR 701)
> 701.8a To destroy a permanent, move it from the battlefield to its owner’s graveyard.

> 701.13a To exile an object, move it to the exile zone from wherever it is. See rule 406,
> “Exile.”

> 701.17a For a player to mill a number of cards, that player puts that many cards from the
> top of their library into their graveyard.

> 701.17b A player can’t mill a number of cards greater than the number of cards in their
> library. If given the choice to do so, they can’t choose to take that action. If
> instructed to do so, they mill as many as possible. […]

## One-shot continuous effects lock their set (CR 611.2c)
> 611.2c If a continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability modifies
> the characteristics or changes the controller of any objects, the set of objects it
> affects is determined when that continuous effect begins. After that point, the set won’t
> change. (Note that this works differently than a continuous effect from a static
> ability.) […]

`Effect::ApplyAll` picks the matching permanents as it resolves and stores them; a creature
that arrives later isn't affected (Terror of Mount Velus, Noxious Ghoul).

## "Where X is …" is worked out once (CR 608.2h)
> 608.2h If an effect requires information from the game (such as the number of creatures on
> the battlefield), the answer is determined only once, when the effect is applied. […]

`Effect::SetX(amount)` evaluates the amount once and later instructions of the same
resolution use `Amount::X` (Chain Reaction, Graveborn Muse), rather than counting again for
each instruction.

## Intervening "if" (CR 603.4, already in 603-triggered-abilities.md)
`Condition::Control` ("if you control six or more lands", "if you control a creature with
power 4 or greater") is checked when the event occurs and again on resolution, like the
life conditions.

## Cost reductions (CR 601.2f, 118.7) — D-043
> 601.2f The player determines the total cost of the spell. Usually this is just the mana
> cost. Some spells have additional or alternative costs. Some effects may increase or
> reduce the cost to pay, or may provide other alternative costs. […] The total cost is the
> mana cost or alternative cost (as determined in rule 601.2b), plus all additional costs
> and cost increases, and minus all cost reductions. If multiple cost reductions apply, the
> player may apply them in any order. If the mana component of the total cost is reduced to
> nothing by cost reduction effects, it is considered to be {0}. It can’t be reduced to less
> than {0}. Once the total cost is determined, any effects that directly affect the total
> cost are applied. Then the resulting total cost becomes “locked in.” If effects would
> change the total cost after this time, they have no effect.

> 118.7a Effects that reduce a cost by an amount of generic mana affect only the generic
> mana component of that cost. They can’t affect the colored or colorless mana components
> of that cost.

Engine: `cast::cost_reduction` adds up "[these] spells you cast cost {N} less" from
permanents the caster controls (only abilities they currently have, invariant 4) and the
spell's own "this spell costs {N} less"; `total` takes it off the generic part only, after
adding commander tax, never below nothing. It is worked out when the cast is announced,
with the tax; nothing can change the board between then and payment.

## "You may", and targets that are cards in a graveyard — D-044
`Effect::May`: the controller decides as the effect resolves (the targets were chosen when
the spell or ability was put on the stack, CR 601.2c / 603.3d — a "may" doesn't make
targeting optional). `TargetFilter::CardInGraveyard` targets a card by what it is
everywhere (its card types and subtypes); it becomes an illegal target if it leaves that
graveyard (it would be a new object, CR 400.7).

## "It" — the object that triggered the ability (CR 608.2h) — D-045
> 608.2h […] If the effect requires information from a specific object, including the
> source of the ability itself, the effect uses the current information of that object if
> it’s in the public zone it was expected to be in; if it’s no longer in that zone, or if
> the effect has moved it from a public zone to a hidden zone, the effect uses the object’s
> last known information. See rule 113.7a. If an ability states that an object does
> something, it’s the object as it exists—or as it most recently existed—that does it, not
> the ability.

A triggered ability remembers the object whose event triggered it (`PendingTrigger::subject`
→ `StackObject::subject`). `ObjRef::Triggering` is that object while it is still on the
battlefield; `Amount::TriggeringPower` / `TriggeringToughness` read it now or as it last
existed; `DealDamage { by: Triggering }` makes it — not the ability's source — the source of
the damage (Warstorm Surge: the creature deals the damage, so its lifelink or deathtouch
would apply, and it still deals it if it has died in response).
