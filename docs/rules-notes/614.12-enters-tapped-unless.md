# CR 614.1c–d, 614.12 — "enters tapped unless …", "as this enters, you may …" (D-067)

- **Source:** Comprehensive Rules effective **November 6, 2026** (local `docs/rules/MagicCompRules.txt`, not committed).
- **Milestone:** M4 (Forge's `R:Event$ Moved` replacement effects)
- Builds on `614-616-replacement-prevention.md` (the pipeline, D-024).

## Verbatim text

> 614.1c Effects that read “[This permanent] enters with . . . ,” “As [this permanent] enters . . . ,” or “[This permanent] enters as . . . “ are replacement effects.

> 614.1d Continuous effects that read “[This permanent] enters . . .” or “[Objects] enter [the battlefield] . . .” are replacement effects.

> 614.12. Some replacement effects modify how a permanent enters the battlefield. (See rules 614.1c–d.) Such effects may come from the permanent itself if they affect only that permanent (as opposed to a general subset of permanents that includes it). They may also come from other sources. To determine which replacement effects apply and how they apply, check the characteristics of the permanent as it would exist on the battlefield, taking into account replacement effects that have already modified how it enters the battlefield (see rule 616.1), continuous effects from the permanent’s own static abilities that would apply to it once it’s on the battlefield, and continuous effects that already exist and would apply to the permanent.
>
> Example: Orb of Dreams is an artifact that says “Permanents enter tapped.” It won’t affect itself, so Orb of Dreams enters the battlefield untapped.

> 614.12a If a replacement effect that modifies how a permanent enters the battlefield requires a choice, that choice is made before the permanent enters the battlefield.

> 614.12b If multiple replacement effects that require choices from a player would modify how multiple permanents enter the battlefield simultaneously, that player may not make choices for those effects that would cause the combined costs of those effects to not be payable.

> 614.1a Effects that use the word “instead” are replacement effects. Most replacement effects use the word “instead” to indicate what events will be replaced with other events.

> 614.13. An effect that modifies how a permanent enters the battlefield may cause other objects to change zones.
>
> 614.13a While applying an effect that modifies how a permanent enters the battlefield, you may have to choose a number of objects that will also change zones. You can’t choose the object that will become that permanent or any other object entering or leaving any zone at the same time that object is entering.

> 701.9a To discard a card, move it from its owner’s hand to that player’s graveyard.

> 603.6d Some permanents have text that reads “[This permanent] enters with . . . ,” “As [this permanent] enters . . . ,” “[This permanent] enters as . . . ,” or “[This permanent] enters tapped.” Such text is a static ability—not a triggered ability—whose effect occurs as part of the event that puts the permanent onto the battlefield.

> 110.5b Permanents enter the battlefield untapped, unflipped, face up, and phased in unless a spell or ability says otherwise.

> 109.5. The words “you” and “your” on an object refer to the object’s controller, its would-be controller (if a player is attempting to play, cast, or activate it), or its owner (if it has no controller). For a static ability, this is the current controller of the object it’s on. […]

> 110.2. […] A permanent’s controller is, by default, the player under whose control it entered the battlefield. Every permanent has a controller.

> 118.3. A player can’t pay a cost without having the necessary resources to pay it fully. For example, a player with only 1 life can’t pay a cost of 2 life, and a permanent that’s already tapped can’t be tapped to pay a cost. […]

> 119.4. If a cost or effect allows a player to pay an amount of life greater than 0, the player may do so only if their life total is greater than or equal to the amount of the payment. If a player pays life, the payment is subtracted from their life total; in other words, the player loses that much life.

> 701.20a To reveal a card, show that card to all players for a brief time. […]
>
> 701.20b Revealing a card doesn’t cause it to leave the zone it’s in.

> 102.2. In a two-player game, a player’s opponent is the other player.
>
> 102.3. In a multiplayer game between teams, a player’s teammates are the other players on their team, and the player’s opponents are all players not on their team.

## What follows for the engine

- **No stack, no priority** (603.6d, 614.12a): "As this enters, you may pay 2 life. If you
  don't, it enters tapped" is a replacement effect. The choice is made while the event is
  being modified, inside the replacement pipeline (`frames::ApplyFrame` suspends), before
  the permanent is on the battlefield.
- **Who chooses** (109.5, 110.2): "you" is the player the permanent will enter under the
  control of — the event's controller, else its owner.
- **Paying life** (119.4, 118.3): only a player with at least that much life is offered
  the payment. The payment is losing life, so it is a replaceable event of its own
  (`Proposed::LoseLife` through the pipeline) and can trigger "whenever you lose life".
  It is paid as the choice is made, before the permanent enters — so for several
  permanents entering at once, the second choice sees the life already paid (614.12b).
- **Revealing** (701.20): the card stays in the hand; all players see it (`Event::Revealed`).
  The card that is entering, and any card moving in the same event, is not offered: for a
  discard that is 614.13a; for a reveal no rule says so, and no card needs it (no reveal
  land asks for a card of its own types) — read by analogy, so nothing can be revealed
  for itself.
- **"If this would enter, you may discard a land card instead. If you do, put it onto the
  battlefield. If you don't, put it into its owner's graveyard."** (614.1a, 614.12a,
  614.13a): the discard is chosen as the event is modified and happens (a replaceable
  event, `MoveCause::Discard`) before the permanent enters; not discarding — or having
  nothing to discard — changes the event's destination to its owner's graveyard.
- **The condition** ("unless you control an Island") is judged as the event is modified:
  the battlefield as it is before the permanent enters. The entering permanent is not on
  the battlefield, so it never counts itself; permanents entering in the same event are
  not on the battlefield either (the batch is performed together, after all events of it
  are modified), so they don't see each other. "Two or more other lands" is therefore
  "two or more lands" counted before it enters.
- **Entering tapped is not becoming tapped** (110.5b): the permanent is tapped as it
  enters; no "becomes tapped" event happens.
- **Other permanents' "enter tapped"** (614.12, example): an effect of a permanent on the
  battlefield applies to others entering; a permanent never applies its own "permanents
  enter tapped" to itself (it isn't on the battlefield yet), nor does a permanent entering
  at the same time.
- **"Opponents"** (102.2–3): players still in the game who are not you.
